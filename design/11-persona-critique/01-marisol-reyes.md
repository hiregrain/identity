# Marisol Reyes, 31, Bakersfield

Eight years of work: two at a poultry processing plant, three on a food
manufacturing line where she became a line lead, overlapping weekend work
at a cousin's dry goods stall paid in cash, two as a warehouse
coordinator, now nine months as a shift supervisor. No degree. Every job
came through someone she knew; every cold application was ignored. No
resume, and the file a friend once typed is lost. Reads English fluently,
skims or quits when text gets heavy. Three-year-old Android, prepaid
data. Left the poultry plant after a dispute about unpaid hours.

Wants to be taken seriously for jobs that pay more without having to know
somebody first. Skeptical of anything that sounds like a credit score,
and quietly afraid of anything permanent that a former employer gets to
write about her.

Evidence tier: synthetic. Binds nothing. See [`README.md`](README.md).

## Walkthrough

**Consent.** "Seven things, all of them true before you add anything."
Seven, before I have done anything. Then "It is permanent, and you cannot
edit it." I have not typed my name yet and I am being told a company
writes about me and I cannot change it. The next one is worse: "Asking
for confirmation cannot be undone." I left the poultry plant over hours
they did not pay me. The man who would be asked to sign for me is the man
I argued with. This screen is telling me he gets a permanent say and I
get none.

I do respect that it says it out loud. Nobody has ever told me the terms
up front like this. Respect is not the same as signing.

I do not understand "chapter", which the screen defines once and then
keeps making me translate. "Imprint" appears with no explanation at all
and the screen assumes I know it. On the phone I see four of the seven
and the button is already sitting there, dark and ready. I would tap it
without reading the rest, and the three I would skip are the ones about
deletion.

The button says "Agree and continue". Not "I understand". Agree.

**Handle.** Short, which I appreciate after that wall. But it is my full
legal name in the address, already filled in, and the button says "Claim
this address" like I am getting a prize. My old supervisor can type my
name and find this. And "The old address keeps working and never goes to
anyone else" means the old one also never dies. Nobody asked me if I
wanted a public page. This screen assumes it.

**Empty.** Clean and honest, and the drawing is pretty. "Work nobody has
confirmed is drawn plain. It fills in when someone who was there signs
for it." So there is a picture of me and right now it is the empty
version. Eight years of work and the app draws me as a grid with nothing
in it. Two buttons, and the second one is not for me because I do not
have a resume. The first means typing five jobs on a phone. I would do
it, but not tonight.

**Imported.** The big number is 5 and next to it "chapters, none of them
confirmed". At the bottom, in the bar I cannot scroll away, "Nothing here
is confirmed, and none of it counts until it is."

None of it counts. I just gave you my whole working life and you told me
it does not count. Five dashes down the left. The ring above is entirely
dotted. The button says "Commit these 5 chapters", and commit is a word
from court, and I already know from the first screen that committing is
the part that cannot be undone.

This is the screen where I put the phone down. Maximum work from me, zero
payoff, and a warning label.

**Main.** Now it looks good. "Cebu Pacific signed your work since you
last looked." Somebody wrote something about me and it was not a
paycheck. First time in this app I felt anything but nervous. The ring is
drawn in and it is genuinely beautiful. I do not know what it means.
Bigger seems better. I cannot tell you what any single wobble is.

On the phone the ring takes the entire screen and my actual work history
is all below it. "Outstanding verification" is a phrase from a bill
collector, and three of my five jobs are in a list of things that are
wrong with me. Then "Work history" repeats all of it again with longer
sentences. Same jobs, twice, in one screen, on my data plan.

The one that hurts is "A coworker signed this. The business is not
registered with Grain." My cousin's stall. Real work, weekends, three
years. The app says the business does not exist. To an employer that
looks like I made it up and got a friend to cover for me.

At the very bottom: "Deleting your whole record is the only way out." So
the poultry line is stuck on me next to my name until I burn the whole
thing down.

**Expanded.** Seven rows, each with a little bar chart partly filled and
partly grey. Those are bars. Bars are a score. I know a score when I see
one. Two of mine are low and I did not pick them; Sunrise Foods picked
them, and my supervisor there barely knew my name.

Under every one of the seven is the exact same three-line paragraph. I
read it once, did not follow it, and scrolled past six copies. At the
bottom is the sentence that would break this for me if I ever got that
far: "They have not been tested for whether two managers reading the same
work pick the same one." So the bars next to my name are one manager's
guess and you never checked whether the guess repeats. That is buried at
the bottom of a screen almost nobody reaches. Also, I do not know what a
lobe is.

**Landing.** The best moment in the app and the one that scares me most
on reflection. Something good is happening to me and all I can do is
watch it. "Play it again", like a slot machine paying out. The header
still counts my failures.

**Sharing.** "Your public page is on." On. I never turned it on. Two
businesses are reading me right now, and one of them is a place I used to
work, holding a live grant to my whole current record including where I
work now. I do not remember agreeing to that and this screen does not
tell me how it happened.

The three middle rows are the clearest thing in the app: Always, Always,
Never. That I understand instantly. But Always means always, and in my
line of work, if the shift manager finds out you are shopping, you get
the bad schedule. And "Public page, 3 this week" means three strangers
looked at me and I do not know who.

**ShareLink.** Honest to a fault, and unusable. Everything, including the
poultry line and the stall line, and I cannot leave those out, and I
cannot un-send it. I like "Your phone and email, never in a link. They
ask you for those." Realistically I would create the link and then not
send it.

**Grant.** This is what they see, and it is written to make them doubt
me. The last thing an employer reads is a summary of what is missing.
Three sentences and two of them are about absence. If I were the hiring
person I would put this down. "Signed by a coworker, not the business."
Three years of my weekends, labelled as not the business.

**Settings.** "47 entries, and none removed." I did not do forty-seven
things. Something is writing about me more than I am. And I cannot change
my own name or number, I have to message somebody and wait, and I work
nights on a prepaid plan. Good: "Employers see one thing: verified, or
not. They never see the document."

**Public.** One line sorts me into ranks: "Two signed by the employer,
one by a coworker, one dates only, one Liezel added herself." And the
worthless one gets its own paragraph. Three times on this page some
version of "she says so" appears. This page has a phrase for my claims
and a different phrase for their claims, and mine is the weaker one every
time.

**PublicReduced.** This is the Google version, and it is the one that
follows me. The last words on my public, search-visible page are that
nobody confirmed my first job. That is the version an employer sees
before I ever get to talk. And there is a locked, better version of me
whose unlock is making an account, so my page is bait for their signups.

## Adversarial

**1. Every honest thing on the record reads as an accusation, and the
honesty is aimed at the person, not the employer.** The vocabulary is
asymmetric. Party claims get neutral verbs. Mine get suspicion verbs:
"Liezel added this. Nobody has confirmed it", "as Liezel gave it". The
employer is never marked as unverified. Only I am. The design says it
refuses to score people and then ships a page whose one-line summary is a
ranked tally of my credibility. The unconfirmed marks are not distributed
at random: they fall on informal workers, which is the population being
sold on this.

**2. All-or-nothing sharing hands the worst thing about me to everyone,
forever, and I know it before I start.** The system's answer to a chapter
that hurts me is delete eight years or live with it. That is not worker
ownership, it is shared custody where the employer's line is permanent
and my only right is arson. And it is stated on screen one, before I have
seen a single benefit.

**3. The seven measures are a score, I read them as a score, and the
design admits inside itself that they may not be reproducible.** A
partially filled bar next to a level word is a credit report. If I read
the footnote the bars are worthless and I know it. If I do not, I carry a
number I did not choose and cannot contest. The one I will actually have
is the second, because the footnote is at the bottom of a screen that
repeats the same paragraph seven times first.

**4. The public page is on by default and the person most likely to see
it is my current boss.** A name-indexed page listing my current employer
and "Jan 2025 present" is a resignation letter I did not write. "Public
page, 3 this week" gives me no way to know whether that was a recruiter
or my manager. And the URL is permanent even after I change it.

**5. I will game it by omission and the design has no defence and no
acknowledgment.** Only add jobs that will sign. Never add poultry. Never
add the stall, because coworker-signed looks worse than silence. The
record becomes a curated highlight reel, which is the resume, which is
the thing being replaced. And the design cannot correct it without
correcting it against me, because gap detection turns unexplained years
into a new accusation.

**6. "None of it counts until it is" is said at the exact moment I have
done the most work.** There is no first reward. Nothing is mine to look
at until a third party acts, and the third party is a company that has
never done anything for me and has no reason to start. Week two is where
I see the same dotted ring and stop opening the app.

**7. Consent gives me the terms and gives the employer the leverage.** I
cannot edit, undo a request, share in part, or delete in part. What a
reader keeps, they keep. Businesses can decline to change it and Grain
never checks whether they were fair. My rights: attach a side note, with
a deadline, that the business can ignore. I am told this product is owned
by me. What the seven describe is a system where the employer writes,
signs and declines, and I get a comment box and a demolition switch.

**8. Free forever, and the money comes from analysing me.** "They can pay
us to analyse what they read", at the end of a paragraph whose heading
promised control. I am skeptical of credit scores because credit scores
are somebody else's business model running on my life. Anyone who reads
that line carefully has a reason not to sign up.

**9. I cannot operate my own account.** Export is by email and my email
is not added. Who has read my record routes to support. Every account
change is a support request. A worker-owned record where the worker has
to email a company to change her own phone number is an account at a
company.

**10. Forty-seven entries I did not make,** unexplained, next to a
promise that every mistake about me is permanent with the fix stapled
beside it. To someone afraid of a permanent record an employer
contributes to, that number is the scariest thing in Settings.

**11. Vocabulary I cannot use out loud.** Chapter, imprint, attested,
grant, outstanding verification, counterparty exposure, lobe. I have to
learn seven words to describe my own work history and I cannot repeat any
of them to a hiring manager without sounding like I am reading from a
card.

**12. "Identity verified" on my screen, "which says nothing about the
work below" on everyone else's.** The one badge I am proud of is
downgraded the moment somebody else looks. I will screenshot the first
version and be confused when it does not carry.

## The three things that would decide it

1. **The record marks my honesty as doubt, in public, permanently.** Keep
   unconfirmed chapters off the indexable page by default and describe
   them neutrally as dates I recorded, so the absence of an employer
   signature is not rendered as a statement about my character.

2. **I cannot separate the one job that can hurt me from the seven years
   that help me.** Let a chapter be withheld from sharing without
   deleting it from the ledger, and show the reader that something was
   withheld rather than forcing an all-or-nothing send.

3. **I do the entire work of onboarding and the app returns a zero.**
   Make the self-entered history worth something on its own, a real page
   I can send the day I sign up, with signatures presented as
   strengthening it rather than as the precondition for it existing.
