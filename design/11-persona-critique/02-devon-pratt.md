# Devon Pratt, 23

Communications degree from a mid-tier state school, then a twelve-week
data analytics bootcamp. Three summers at a coffee chain, a semester as a
campus tour guide, a four-month marketing internship at a startup that
shut down and whose founders no longer answer email, six months
contracting for a small agency, currently part-time at a coworking front
desk. 180 applications, four interviews. iPhone, fluent with apps, taps
everything, reads almost nothing.

His real anxiety is that his record is thin and everyone can tell. He has
spent two years learning to make a resume look fuller than it is. His
entire applied skill set is impression management, and this product
appears to take it away.

Evidence tier: synthetic. Binds nothing. See [`README.md`](README.md).

## Walkthrough

**Consent.** That is a wall. I am not reading seven things. I scroll to
the button and the button is already pinned at the bottom, so the scroll
was pointless. Two headings caught me on the way down because they are
bold and short: "Businesses write about your work" and "It is permanent,
and you cannot edit it." Then the one that stops me: "There is no way to
show them part of it."

That is the whole product, on the terms screen, before I have typed
anything. On LinkedIn I control the edit. Here a business writes about me
permanently and I cannot hide the bad one. I would still tap agree,
because I always do, but I am now reading this as a background check I am
volunteering for, not a resume. "They can pay us to analyse what they
read" is sitting inside a grey paragraph. If I had read it I would have
closed the app.

The four items that scare me are 1, 2, 3 and 6. The one that would
reassure me is buried at 5 and immediately undercut by "they can decline
to change it".

**Handle.** Fine. Two seconds. The one thing I like: "The old address
keeps working and never goes to anyone else." This is the only screen so
far that feels like it is on my side.

**Empty.** Rough headline for a guy whose entire problem is that he has
nothing on it yet. But the figure is clearly going to become something
and I want to see it become something. That is the first pull the app has
had on me. Obviously I import the resume, because my resume is already
tuned. This is where I realise the app is going to take my carefully
managed document and start asking other people about it.

**Imported.** Above the fold: a dotted ring, "none attested", a huge 5
next to "none of them confirmed", and "Outstanding verification 5." Every
single thing above the fold is a zero. Nothing says "nice, you have five
jobs". It says five times that I have five liabilities.

Then "none of it counts until it is". My resume, the object I have spent
two years polishing, ingested and graded as zero. And the button says
commit, which plus "cannot be undone" makes me hesitate for the first
time in this whole flow, and I have not hesitated on an app in years.
Because I know what happens next: Grain emails a startup that no longer
exists and a coffee chain manager who does not remember me.

What I do not understand: can I delete the four-month internship before
committing? Five rows, no per-row control, no swipe, no X. I would tap a
row. If tapping does nothing, I put the phone down here. Highest-risk
screen in the app and it is at step four.

**Main.** The ring is the best thing in the product. It looks like
something. If mine looked like that I would screenshot it and put it on
LinkedIn. That is the actual hook and it takes zero reading.

But the first section under it is what is wrong with me, and the
sentences are brutal in a way no product I use is brutal. "As you gave
it" is on my current job. A public asterisk on my job title that says do
not trust this man. And the small grey line at the bottom means I have to
nuke the entire account to remove one bad entry. That sentence decides
whether I ever put a real employer on here.

The little marks at the left of each row: some are dashes, some are
little arcs, one is a solid line. Nothing on this screen tells me what
they mean. I would tap one.

Exposed? Completely. This screen is a truth serum with a nice ring on
top.

**Expanded.** Where I actually stop and read, which is rare. Good,
tappable, I will tap all seven. This is the part I would try to
influence. It is a bar meter per category, which is a score, which means
there is a right answer and I want it. The phrasing I do not understand
on first read and would not read twice. The bars I understand instantly.
So I am optimising for bars whose definitions I cannot see.

Under every measure is the identical paragraph, seven times. I stopped
seeing it after the first, which is a shame, because it contains the most
gameable fact in the app: highest level two parties assert wins, never an
average.

And at the bottom Grain says out loud that the anchors were never tested
for whether two managers pick the same one. So the meter is admittedly
noisy and drawn as a precise meter that drives a picture. That is the gap
I would live in.

**Landing.** The one dopamine moment in the app and it is well done. I
would want to send this as a video. There is not one.

**Sharing.** My reaction to the opening line is not gratitude, it is
alarm. Two businesses are reading my whole record right now and I did not
feel like I turned that on. The values I actually parse are Always,
Always, Never. So the public page permanently advertises which of my jobs
nobody confirmed, and there is no toggle next to those rows. My only
control is all-or-nothing. Given my history, off.

"Public page, 3 this week" is the only number here that could keep me in
the app.

**ShareLink.** Clean, and the dark box is the only place in the app that
raises its voice. I understand it. I would never use it, because there is
no version of this link I want a recruiter to open.

**Grant.** The first time I see myself from their side. The recruiter's
eye goes straight down the second lines, and one of five is clean. A
recruiter looking at this for eleven seconds sees a column of caveats and
moves to the next candidate whose column has fewer. And the footer
summarises the caveats again for anyone who did not read the caveats.

**Settings.** Better than fine in two places. The append-only line is a
strong signal that this thing is real, and "They never see the document"
is the most reassuring sentence in the app, buried in settings.

**Public.** Strongest screen visually. That one summary sentence is the
whole product. It is also a rating: it sorts me instantly against every
other candidate, and there is no way for me to raise it, because for
three of my six things the people are gone.

**PublicReduced.** The ring is gone and the caveats stay. The thing that
makes me look good is behind a sign-in wall and the thing that makes me
look thin is fully public and indexable. For a person with a thin record
the logged-out page is strictly worse than having no page at all. This is
where I turn the public page off and probably stop opening the app.

## Adversarial

**1. The public page shows my liabilities and hides my asset, and the
only control is a master switch.** The rows with no toggle are the ones
that stay Always; the imprint, which is the flattering half, is the one
that gets gated. Every thin-record user rationally turns the page off,
which means the public surface fills with strong records only and becomes
exactly the credential screen the product says it is not.

**2. Not committing is free, so the record becomes a highlight reel by
omission.** Adding is permanent; never adding costs nothing, and no
screen shows a gap, a date range with no chapter, or a count of years
unaccounted for. I commit two chapters and leave three off. My Grain
record then reads better than my honest resume. The product punishes the
honest user, who commits everything and gets three rows of "nobody has
confirmed it" next to the two good ones.

**3. The measures are a five-bar score with no published rubric, so I
coach the signer.** A meter that admits it is noisy still renders as a
score, and the person filling it in is a manager I am currently on good
terms with who has no stake in being conservative. Main even instructs me
to do this: "Ask them to say what you did."

**4. Highest of two, never an average, is a stated instruction for how to
stack signers.** I need exactly two friendly signers to lock a high level
and a third honest one cannot pull it down. And coworkers can sign, so
the two friendly parties do not both have to be the business. Two
agreeable coworkers beat one accurate manager, permanently, with no path
to correction.

**5. The employer view is a caveat column, and no recruiter reads the
caveats, only counts them.** A screener does not adjudicate nuance, they
count clean rows and rank. The design assumes reading and delivers
ranking. "The partner decides what it means" is done in eleven seconds by
counting.

**6. Nothing here is a resume, and I would never send it instead of
one.** My resume has bullets with numbers in them. Grain has "as you gave
it" and explicitly says it never checks whether the work was done well.
So Grain removes my only lever, narrative, and adds nothing a recruiter
asked for. I keep the app dormant, page off, and never share the link,
which makes me a dead account rather than a churned one.

**7. Deletion is the only eraser and it is a support ticket.** One bad
attestation from a manager I fell out with, and my only remedy is asking
a company to destroy the whole thing, after which the bad chapter still
lives in a recruiter's file. That is what makes me delete the app, and it
happens at the moment I most need the product to be on my side.

**8. Timing is unmanaged and works in my favour.** No screen shows a
deadline for the business signing, so I control when a request goes out.
I request the good employer now and the risky one, if ever, the day after
a grant expires. Outstanding verification is a list I keep at zero by
never asking.

**9. "Identity verified" on Main is a badge the employer view carefully
defuses.** If I screenshot Main, and I will, it travels alone as a green
check with none of its qualification.

**10. Consent buries the monetisation and the frame hides it.** The one
commercial fact a user would object to is placed inside the item whose
heading promises control.

**11. An unverifiable business is still a scoreable one.** A business
that does not exist on Grain still yields a signed chapter through an
individual. My dead startup is not actually unattestable, I just need one
former colleague with a phone, and nothing distinguishes an arms-length
coworker from my roommate.

**12. "Play it again" is the only shareable moment and it is not
shareable.** The one artifact I would voluntarily post is trapped, while
the two share affordances that exist are the two things I never want
seen.

## The three things that would decide it

1. **The logged-out page keeps every unconfirmed row and drops the
   ring.** Gate the unconfirmed rows behind sign-in as well, or show the
   imprint to logged-out readers, so the public surface is not
   systematically unflattering to exactly the people who need it.

2. **Omission is free while inclusion is permanent.** Show unaccounted
   time, or state on the reader-facing surfaces that the record contains
   only what the worker chose to enter.

3. **The seven measures are a scored ladder with an unpublished rubric
   and a documented stacking rule.** Publish the rule for each level
   inside the signing flow so signer and worker see the same definition,
   and either require the two agreeing parties to be independent or stop
   rendering the result as a meter.
