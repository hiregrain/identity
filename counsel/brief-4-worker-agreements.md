# Counsel engagement brief 4: worker onboarding agreements, consent architecture, verification-freeze enforceability, lawful basis, and FCRA positioning

**Jurisdictions:** United States and Philippines first (launch markets); EU/India deferred except where noted
**Date prepared:** 2026-08-17
**Status of this document:** Instructions to counsel. Internal research memos cited are not legal advice and have not been counsel-reviewed; source URLs are provided for independent verification.

---

## 1. Background: what the company and product are

The company operates a **persistent worker identity and work-attestation ledger**. Anyone may self-sign up and receive a permanent platform identity; workers then accumulate a record composed of (a) **self-asserted claims** (worker-authored, editable until third-party verified), (b) **peer attestations**, and (c) **party attestations**, cryptographically signed statements by registered attesting parties (employers, training providers; initially the company's own operating verticals, later external partners), about work performed. Workers control disclosure at the **party level**: they grant and revoke which parties may read their record, but do not curate which claims a permitted reader sees. Every read is logged and worker-visible. Sensitive data categories (government ID numbers, DOB, education records beyond credential name/issuer, health, criminal matters) are banned from attestation payloads by schema.

Two founder rulings fix the posture and must be reflected, and stress-tested, in the onboarding instrument:

- **R1: worker-owned platform, not a CRA.** The worker owns the record fundamentally; the product is the worker's portable verified resume and world identity, with partners integrating against that identity layer. The founder's operating model is the worker-published-profile theory under which platforms like LinkedIn have survived FCRA challenge. **Counsel should know that our internal research reached the contrary conclusion** (detailed in §3 below) and that the founder directed both positions be put before counsel neutrally. Evaluating this is part of this engagement.
- **R2: whole-profile deletion yes, record editing no.** The worker may delete their entire profile at any time (total deletion is a first-class right). But once a self-asserted claim is third-party verified, it **freezes**: the worker can never again edit it, and party attestations are append-only from birth. Disputes end in a superseding correction by the issuer or a permanent, co-rendered worker counter-statement, never worker edits. The worker is warned of the freeze at the moment they request verification.

The onboarding agreement is therefore an unusual instrument: it must obtain durable, informed worker consent to (1) receipt of append-only attestations from parties the worker engages with, (2) the verification-freeze, (3) the party-level (not claim-level) disclosure model, (4) the dispute process as the exclusive in-record remedy, and (5) it must deliver the total-deletion right, while surviving the fact that most workers will encounter it in an employment-adjacent context where their bargaining power is weak.

## 2. Questions presented

1. **Instrument architecture.** For US and PH launch: should worker onboarding be one agreement or a layered set (terms of service + separate consent instrument + per-verification just-in-time consents)? PH DPA Sec. 3(b) requires consent "freely given, specific, informed" and "evidenced by written, electronic or recorded means"; our research concludes a single click-through TOS will not carry Sec. 13(a)'s "specific to the purpose, prior to the processing" for sensitive personal information, and that consent records must be durable, per-purpose, timestamped, and versioned against the notice text shown. Confirm the required structure and produce the consent-capture specification the product must implement.
2. **Verification-freeze enforceability.** Is the freeze ("you may never edit this record again once verified; your remedies are dispute, counter-statement, or whole-profile deletion") enforceable against a consumer/worker under (a) US state consumer-protection and unconscionability doctrines (procedural: adhesion contract at signup; substantive: permanent loss of control over one's own profile content), and (b) PH consumer and civil-law doctrines? What disclosure, timing (at signup vs. at each verification request), and formatting (separate signature/checkbox, plain-language notice) does counsel require to make it stick? Is there any jurisdiction where a worker's later demand to edit a verified record could be enforced against the platform notwithstanding the agreement?
3. **Lawful basis mix, Philippines.** Employment-context consent is weak in PH doctrine. The prompt for this engagement flags NPC Advisory Opinion No. 2019-034 (employer-employee consent treated as generally not freely given; **counsel to pull and confirm**, as our corpus verified the parallel GDPR position but not this opinion's text), and IRR Sec. 34(b)'s objection-right exception for processing "necessary or desirable in the context of an employer-employee relationship" does **not** help a third-party platform that is not the worker's employer. Meanwhile: ordinary personal information can rest on Sec. 12(b) contract-with-the-data-subject and Sec. 12(f) legitimate interests (which expressly extends to third-party recipients' interests, with NPC Circular 2023-07's documented three-part LIA and per-recipient verification); sensitive personal information has **no legitimate-interest basis**. Sec. 13(a) specific prior consent is effectively the only lane, and it is revocable with erasure reach-back under IRR 34(e)(d). Given a **worker-owned platform where the worker initiates signup and controls disclosure**: what lawful basis mix does counsel recommend per data category and processing operation (profile hosting, attestation receipt, disclosure to grantee parties, verification events), so that the record does not stand or fall on revocable consent? Does the worker-initiated, worker-controlled character rehabilitate consent quality here in a way employer-obtained consent cannot?
4. **Lawful basis, US/GDPR cross-check (limited).** For the US the instrument is contractual; for the EU (deferred market) our research concluded consent is the wrong primary basis (Art. 29 WP Opinion 2/2017 power-imbalance analysis) and Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interests is the realistic basis. No EU drafting is requested now; counsel should only flag any US-instrument choice that would foreclose the intended EU basis later.
5. **FCRA/ICRAA risk positioning: evaluate, do not assume.** Present-tense facts: partner companies will pay to read worker records ("prior packets") and use them in hiring, promotion, retention, and task-allocation decisions. Our internal research memo concluded the US product **is** a consumer reporting agency on these facts: 15 U.S.C. §1681a(f) satisfied element-by-element; §1681a(h) reaching incumbent-employer reads; the §1681a(o) staffing exclusion failing on (o)(1) (covers only interview-derived investigative reports), (o)(4) (single-use limitation forecloses a reusable record), and (o)(2) (prospective employers only); and, in the memo's sharpest point, worker consent, visibility, and permissioning *strengthening* the CRA case because each maps onto an existing CRA duty (§1681b(a)(2), §1681g, §1681e(a)). It further flagged California ICRAA as stricter ($10,000/violation; "any means," *Connor v. First Student*, 5 Cal.5th 1026 (2018)). The founder's contrary position (R1): the worker-published-profile model, under which the worker authors/owns the profile and directs its publication, as with LinkedIn, takes the platform outside CRA status, and known platforms' survival of FCRA challenge is the model to build on. **Questions:** (a) Which position is right on our facts? Specifically, does the worker-published-profile theory survive when the published content includes *third-party-authored, platform-solicited, signed attestations* rather than worker-authored content, and when reads are sold to employers for employment purposes? (b) If CRA risk is material, what is the minimum set of contract terms and product constraints that most reduces it without abandoning R1 (e.g., worker-directed distribution mechanics, no platform-derived scores to readers, reader certifications)? (c) What willfulness-exposure discipline should govern our internal documents, given that a written internal conclusion of exemption later rejected is §1681n willfulness evidence? (d) State overlays beyond California that change the answer.
6. **Minors and age verification.** Workers may be 16–17 in some launch verticals and jurisdictions. (a) US: what age floor and parental-consent mechanics does counsel require for an employment-records platform (COPPA does not reach 13+, but state minor-consent, disaffirmance-of-contract, and minor-data statutes may)? A minor's power to disaffirm contracts bears directly on the enforceability of the verification-freeze and the covenant structure in Brief 3. Address specifically. (b) PH: DPA treatment of minors' consent, age of majority (18), and whether workers aged 15–17 (lawfully employable with restrictions) can validly consent to the instrument, or require parental/guardian consent. (c) What age-verification obligation attaches at signup in each jurisdiction, and what is the minimum-intrusive compliant design (self-declaration vs. verified DOB, noting DOB is sensitive personal information under PH Sec. 3(l)(1) and is banned from attestation payloads; any age-verification datum must live in the segregated consent-gated store)?
7. **Deliverables.** (a) US worker agreement + consent instrument; (b) PH worker agreement + consent instrument (English; advise whether Filipino translation is required or advisable for validity); (c) the consent-capture and notice-versioning product specification; (d) a signed memo on Question 5 suitable for board-level risk review.

## 3. Relevant facts and our current working analysis

**PH consent mechanics.** RA 10173 (https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html), IRR (https://www.officialgazette.gov.ph/2016/08/25/implementing-rules-and-regulations-of-republic-act-no-10173/). Sec. 3(l) defines sensitive personal information broadly, covering education records, government-issued IDs, age/DOB and marital status, health, and criminal proceedings (including alleged offenses), and Sec. 13 prohibits SPI processing outside six narrow cases, with no legitimate-interest lane (confirmed by NPC Circular 2023-07: https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/NPC-Circular-No.-2023-07_Guidelines-on-Legitimate-Interest_13-December-2023.pdf; Sec. 13(f) limits per NPC Advisory 2024-02: https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NPC-Advisory-No.-2024-02-Personal-Data-Processing-Based-on-Section-13-f.pdf). Our design response: SPI is segregated into a separate, consent-gated, individually revocable store, and attestation payloads are SPI-free so they can rest on Sec. 12(b)/(f). The agreement must implement this two-lane basis structure. Consent-quality enforcement is real: the NPC's Tools for Humanity CDO (Oct 2025) found consent vitiated by monetary incentive and an unclear forward-looking notice (https://privacy.gov.ph/npc-issues-cease-and-desist-order-against-tools-for-humanity/). This is directly relevant if signup is ever incentivized. Rights-exercise procedure: NPC Advisory 2021-01, 30-working-day response (https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/NPC-Advisory-2021-01-FINAL.pdf). PH Sec. 16(d) *requires* corrected and retracted versions to remain accessible and travel together to recipients. This is statutory support for the append-only-with-supersession model that the agreement should recite.

**Verification-freeze design facts.** The freeze is disclosed at the moment the worker requests verification (just-in-time warning is already product design, `design/ledger-design-0.1.md` §2.2); prior versions of self-asserted claims are retained while the profile lives; the dispute process (30+15-day window, superseding correction or permanent co-rendered counter-statement) is the in-record remedy; whole-profile deletion is ungated except for an anti-coercion confirmation window measured in days. Deletion purges all payload data within a published window; already-delivered packets are governed by recipient terms; the platform retains only unlinkable cryptographic tombstones (the subject of Brief 2).

**FCRA record, stated for neutrality.** The full analysis is `research/01-regulatory-perimeter.md` §1 (statute: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681a; https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i; https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c). Supporting authorities cited there: *Kidd v. Thomson Reuters Corp.*, 925 F.3d 99 (2d Cir. 2019) (intent-based CRA test, which the memo reads as *satisfied* by this product's design and pitch: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/17-3550/17-3550-2019-05-30.html); FTC 2013 guidance and *Spokeo* consent decree (self-labeling insufficient: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2013/01/background-screening-reports-fcra-just-saying-youre-not-consumer-reporting-agency-isnt-enough; https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2012/06/spokeo-pay-800000-settle-ftc-charges-company-allegedly-marketed-information-employers-recruiters); the 1998 FTC advisory opinions on §603(o) (*Basting*: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/advisory-opinions/advisory-opinion-basting-06-11-98); CFPB Circular 2024-06 (worker dossiers/scores; withdrawn 12 May 2025; the memo treats withdrawal as reduced enforcement probability, not changed statute: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/circulars/consumer-financial-protection-circular-2024-06-background-dossiers-and-algorithmic-scores-for-hiring-promotion-and-other-employment-decisions/; withdrawal: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/05/12/2025-08286/interpretive-rules-policy-statements-and-advisory-opinions-withdrawal; NCLC on continued vitality: https://library.nclc.org/article/continued-vitality-67-withdrawn-cfpb-guidance-documents); ICRAA and *Connor* (https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-supreme-court/1948536.html). The founder's position rests on the observed survival of worker-published-profile platforms; the design deliberately preserves a cheap pivot (policy-configurable reportability window, FCRA-shaped dispute process, read logging, party-level grants) so that a counsel-driven change is configuration, not schema migration (`design/ledger-design-0.1.md` TLDR-11, §2.6). Neither internal position binds counsel; we are paying for the answer.

**Minors.** Our corpus has *not* researched minor-consent or age-verification law in either jurisdiction; the only adjacent datum is an incident note on the risk of retaining ID images for age-verification purposes (Discord/5CA breach, Oct 2025, in `research/09-idv-vendor-landscape.md`). Question 6 is greenfield for counsel.

## 4. What the answer will drive, and when it is needed

This engagement produces the **worker onboarding agreements for the two launch jurisdictions and the consent-capture product specification**. Nothing ships to a first worker without them, and the consent-capture spec has engineering lead time (notice versioning, per-purpose consent records, SPI store gating). The Question 5 memo drives a live strategic decision the founder has reserved: whether to keep R1's positioning as-is, harden it with counsel's minimum-constraint set, or pivot toward deliberate CRA-native compliance. The design keeps that pivot cheap, but the decision gates partner-contract drafting (reader certifications) and product copy. Question 6 gates the age floor at signup, which is a launch-blocking product parameter for the PH ops verticals. **Needed: before the first worker signs. The whole engagement is on the launch critical path; the Question 5 memo and Question 2 answer are wanted first, within one quarter, because they can force instrument-architecture changes.**
