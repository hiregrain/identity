## 1. Background: what the company and product are

Grain operates a **persistent worker identity and work-attestation ledger**. A worker holds a permanent opaque identity (`ledger_person_id`, issued at self-signup with no proofing gate) and accumulates **signed attestations** about work performed, issued by registered "attesting parties," initially Grain's own operating verticals and later external employers and partner companies. Attestations are Ed25519/JWS-signed over a canonical payload, hash-chain timestamped, append-only (a correction is a superseding attestation, never an edit), and carry an explicit provenance class (`self_asserted | peer_attested | party_attested`). The full field set is `model/attestation-interface.md` (schema_version 0.2 ratified, decision 006; 0.3 proposed).

**What the record contains, stated plainly for a competition lawyer.** The record is **current, person-level and non-anonymised**. Per attestation: the attesting party's identity; the subject; work kind from a ledger-authored controlled vocabulary bound as `term@version`; volume of units completed; responsibility dimensions exercised and at what level; outcome facts (completed, terminated early, extended, renewed, promoted during, accepted without rework, stretch held); a `score_summary` scored against the issuing party's own quality bar; engagement periods; and, on the read side, employment history and tenure across every party that has ever attested. **Compensation is excluded from the schema entirely**. There is no wage, salary, rate, bonus or benefits field anywhere in the attestation or the prior packet, and no plan to add one.

**Who contributes it.** Employers who compete with each other for the same workers in the same local labour market. The first vertical is **hospitality**, where the relevant labour market is a single metropolitan area and the number of meaningful employers is small. Grain's verticals share one record; a customer organisation, an internal vertical and an external partner are schema-identical and distinguishable only by the ledger's party registry (decision 007).

**What is sold.** The record layer is **free to everyone, permanently**, for workers and employers alike (decision 024); the compiled data is the asset, not access to it. Revenue comes from the **analytics layer**: capability and trajectory assessments computed on request across the pooled multi-employer record and sold to employers (decision 022). Analytics ships from day one and, because the record layer is free forever, carries all revenue.

**The controls that we believe distinguish this from a conventional exchange, and which counsel should test rather than accept:**

- **Worker-controlled disclosure.** The record is private by default. No party sees any record except under a party-level grant the worker issues and can revoke. Prior packets are generated at read time under an active grant, never stored, never edge-cached, so revocation and deletion always bind.
- **A party may not see what it did not supply, absent the worker's grant.** There is no employer-facing query over the pooled record, no directory, no reverse lookup. `roster-firewall.md` (0.1-proposed, decision 032) forbids anything derived from an employer's roster crossing into the ledger except through a **worker-initiated** verification request (decision 031).
- **Quality is party-local and never normalised.** Every `score_summary` carries the `bar_ref` of the rubric it was measured against; the ledger publishes no common scale and packets relay no scores finer than coarse ordinal tiers. **Party quality scores never feed grading**. The stated reason is that grading on them would launder other employers' judgments rather than produce independent intelligence (`THESIS.md` §4).
- **Pairwise pseudonyms.** Under proposed 0.3 [A-6] each partner holds a per-partner pseudonym for a subject, meaningless to any other partner, so the record cannot become a cross-service correlation key.
- **Sensitive-data ban.** Government IDs, DOB, health, criminal proceedings, nationality/immigration detail are schema-invalid, enforced at ingestion.

**The structural facts that cut the other way, and which we are not going to minimise:**

- Grain both **holds the record and operates hiring products on it**. It is simultaneously the neutral administrator and a participant in the market it administers. `THESIS.md` §2 concedes that neutrality "is maintained by contract, published rules and audit rather than by corporate separation" (decision 024), calls that "a weaker guarantee than a bureau's", and records **[GAP]** that no trigger has been set for when structural separation becomes necessary (`THESIS.md` §10 item 22).
- **Re-identification of the individual is not an incidental risk; it is the product.** Every aggregation-based safeguard in the standard information-exchange remedy set is unavailable by construction.
- The data is **current**, not lagged. Attestations are written as work completes and the read path is live.
- Coverage is deliberately **thin-market first**: the go-to-market is a single metropolitan hospitality market, which is the market structure in which exchange doctrine is most hostile.

**Pre-launch and pre-revenue.** No external attesting party has been activated. No analytics product has been sold. Nothing described here is in production.

## 2. Questions presented

1. **Is this an information exchange among competitors at all?**
   (a) Under §1 of the Sherman Act, does a pooled, third-party-administered record of **individual workers' employment histories, tenure, outcome facts and evaluative attestations**, containing no wage, price or firm-level output data, constitute an exchange of competitively sensitive information among horizontal competitors in the purchase of labour?
   (b) Same question under Art. 101 TFEU and the Chapter I prohibition, applying the Horizontal Guidelines' "commercially sensitive information" test.
   (c) Does it matter that the unit of exchange is a **person** rather than a **term of employment**? Is there authority either way on worker-level as opposed to wage-level or firm-level exchange?
   (d) Is any part of the arrangement exposed to **by-object / per se** treatment, or does it fall to be assessed by effects throughout?

2. **Does worker-controlled disclosure change the analysis?**
   (a) Is the subject's consent relevant to a competition analysis at all, or does the harm theory run to the employers' side of the transaction irrespective of the worker's wishes? Our working assumption, stated so it can be corrected, is that **worker consent is irrelevant to antitrust**. The protected interest is competition in the labour market, and a worker cannot consent on behalf of the market.
   (b) If consent is irrelevant, does the **architectural** restriction still help: no employer-facing query surface, no reverse lookup, no visibility into a record the employer did not supply absent a worker grant, and revocability at any time? Is "the flow is worker-gated and one-to-one" a materially different structure from a data pool, or the same structure with an access-control layer?
   (c) Does the answer change once the network is dense enough that in a given metropolitan hospitality market **most candidates present a Grain record**, so that a grant becomes a practical condition of being hired and the "voluntariness" of disclosure is nominal?

3. **Does the analytics product create exposure separate from the record layer?**
   (a) Does selling **capability and trajectory assessments computed across a pooled multi-employer dataset** facilitate coordination, even where no wage or compensation data is exchanged and no output is transmitted about any worker the customer has not been granted access to?
   (b) Is the schema's **exclusion of compensation** doing real work, or is a common, cross-employer measure of worker quality a sufficient input to coordinated wage-setting on its own? That is, does supplying the numerator let employers coordinate the denominator?
   (c) Grain's design already refuses to normalise other parties' quality scores and refuses to feed them into grading, for reasons unrelated to antitrust. Does that refusal help, hurt, or neither? Would **publishing a common levelling spine** (the seven responsibility dimensions with ledger-authored level definitions, `THESIS.md` §5) create a focal point for wage benchmarking that the proprietary version would not?
   (d) Does an **ephemeral, computed-on-request** output, not retained as a fact about the person and with only a run record retained, carrying no output (decision 026), improve the position?

4. **Can the Agri Stats remedy set be satisfied in any form here?**
   The May 2026 proposed final judgment is, on our reading, the agencies' current statement of what a competitively sensitive information exchange must look like to be tolerable: no company- or facility-level data, industry metrics no more specific than quartile, information at least 45 days old on average, symmetric availability to buyers on like terms, and an appointed compliance monitor. **Grain can satisfy none of these**, because person-level identification is the product.
   (a) Is any of that remedy set translatable to a per-person portable record, or is it structurally incompatible?
   (b) If incompatible, **what alternative safeguards would counsel expect a court or an agency to accept**? And are there any? Candidates we can imagine, all untested: hard architectural separation between the record plane and any employer-facing analytic; a bar on any output that ranks or compares workers across employers; contractual prohibition on using Grain outputs in compensation-setting; a bar on selling to more than one employer in a defined local market until a coverage threshold is passed; independent audit of query patterns. If none of these is sufficient, we need to hear that.
   (c) Is there a coverage or concentration threshold below which the exposure is not real, and can it be stated operationally (share of local employers, share of local workforce, number of participating employers in a market)?

5. **Does the vertical structure aggravate it?**
   Grain holds the record **and** operates hiring products on it. `THESIS.md` §2 concedes neutrality rests on contract, published rules and audit rather than corporate separation, and sets no trigger for when structural separation becomes necessary.
   (a) Does Grain's own participation as a market participant convert a third-party-administered exchange into something worse (a hub in a hub-and-spoke structure, or a participant with its own incentive to use the pooled data competitively)?
   (b) **What conduct or scale should trigger structural separation** (separate legal entity, separate governance, information barriers, or divestiture of the verticals)? We are asking counsel to specify the trigger, because we have not been able to, and it is recorded as an open gap.
   (c) If Grain ever places trained specialists who compete with the partners whose attestations fill the graph (`THESIS.md` §8), does that change the answer?

6. **Jurisdictional scope.** Please answer 1–5 for **US federal** law and for the **EU** and **UK**, and give a shorter assessment for the **Philippines** (Philippine Competition Act, RA 10667) and **India** (Competition Act 2002 as amended), both of which are on the expansion roadmap and where we have no analysis at all. Specifically: does either regime reach buyer-side/labour-market conduct, and is there any enforcement history against information exchange among employers?

7. **What must be avoided now, at pre-launch, that is cheap today and expensive later?**
   (a) **Data-sharing architecture**: what must be built into the read path, the query surface and the party registry before first external activation.
   (b) **Contractual terms with attesting parties**: what the party agreement must prohibit, and whether an antitrust covenant against use in compensation-setting is worth having or is an admission.
   (c) **What the analytics product may output**, and specifically whether any cross-employer comparative output is safe.
   (d) **Conduct**: what Grain personnel, marketing copy and customer-facing materials must never say. We would specifically like to know whether any of the language in our own public-facing thesis ("the union of what many independent parties attested about one person", "cross-employer record", "network") is language a plaintiff would use against us, and what to say instead that is still true.
   (e) Whether a formal antitrust compliance programme is warranted **now**, pre-revenue.

## 3. Relevant facts and our current working analysis

Counsel should treat everything below as our position to be confirmed or corrected. Confidence is low throughout; this is a first scoping, not a considered view.

**The structural resemblance we are worried about.** Strip away the worker-ownership framing and the arrangement is: competitors in the purchase of labour in a concentrated local market contribute current, firm-attributed, non-anonymised data about individual workers to a common pool administered by a third party, which sells analysis computed on the pool back to the contributors. That is an information exchange among competitors, administered by a third party, in a thin market, with current and disaggregated data. Every adjective in that sentence points the wrong way in the case law.

**Todd v. Exxon Corp., 275 F.3d 191 (2d Cir. 2001): [verified].** Fourteen oil and petrochemical companies controlling 80–90% of the industry's revenues and workforce exchanged salary information for non-union managerial, professional and technical employees through surveys and meetings; the data was compiled, analysed and disseminated by **Towers Perrin, a third-party consultant**. The Second Circuit **vacated the Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal and remanded**, holding that a buyer-side labour market is a cognisable relevant market for §1 purposes and setting out the structural factors for a data-exchange case: market concentration, fungibility of the traded input, elasticity of demand, whether the data is **current or historical**, whether it is **public or private**, and whether it is **firm-specific or aggregated** (https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/275/191/592358/; full opinion text: https://pbwt2.gjassets.com/content/uploads/2022/02/Todd-v-Exxon-Corp.pdf; https://openjurist.org/275/f3d/191/roberta-todd-v-exxon-corporation-).

Our reading, and we want it corrected if wrong: on those six factors Grain's arrangement is **worse than Todd on every one that we can assess**. The data is current rather than historical; private rather than public; firm-specific rather than aggregated; and the first market is a metropolitan hospitality labour market, which is likely more concentrated than the national MPT market in *Todd*. The one factor that cuts our way is that we exchange no compensation data at all. We do not know how much weight that carries, and Question 3(b) is the question we most want answered. **[unverified as a characterisation of the holding]** that third-party administration by Towers Perrin was expressly held not to insulate the defendants. The opinion treats the third-party structure as one circumstance among several rather than a safe harbour, and counsel should read the passage rather than rely on our gloss.

**United States et al. v. Agri Stats, Inc., No. 23-cv-03009 (D. Minn.): [verified].** The United States and six state attorneys general filed a proposed Final Judgment on 7 May 2026, published with the Competitive Impact Statement at 91 Fed. Reg. 34512 (5 June 2026) (https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/05/2026-11329/united-states-et-al-v-agri-stats-inc-proposed-final-judgment-and-competitive-impact-statement; https://regulations.justia.com/regulations/fedreg/2026/06/05/2026-11329.html; case docket summary: https://www.naag.org/multistate-case/united-states-and-plaintiff-states-v-agri-stats-d-minn-023-cv-03009-jrt-jfd/). The complaint alleged that information exchanges Agri Stats operated among competing broiler chicken, pork and turkey processors unreasonably restrained trade under §1.

The remedy set, as reported by practitioner analysis of the decree (https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260512-doj-states-reach-significant-settlement-in-agri-stats-information-sharing-antitrust-litigation; https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/agri-stats-settlement-clarifies-doj-views-on-information-sharing.html): a ban on the Sales Report Books carrying non-public price information; a prohibition on disclosing the identity of contributing processors, on ranking individual processors, and on reporting industry metrics at more specificity than **quartile level**; a requirement that reports contain information **at least 45 days old on average**, rising to 90 days for production-decision data; a requirement that permitted reports be made available to **any interested buyer on like terms** as those provided to processors; an antitrust compliance programme; a **compliance monitor for up to seven years**, with the decree enforceable for ten. Entered without admission of liability and with no monetary payment to the government.

We read this as the agencies' current statement of the tolerable shape of a competitively sensitive information exchange, and **Grain satisfies none of it**. Anonymisation of the contributor is impossible where the contributor's identity is the provenance that makes the record worth anything; quartile-level aggregation is impossible where the output is about one named person; a 45-day lag defeats the hiring use case; symmetric public availability is meaningless for a private per-person record. This is the question we most want a straight answer to, and we would rather hear "structurally incompatible, do not build it this way" now than after the first external party is live. Note one internal shorthand corrected here: we had been describing the monitoring provision as a "monitoring trustee"; it is a **compliance monitor** appointed under the decree, which may or may not matter to how counsel reads it.

**DOJ/FTC Antitrust Guidelines for Business Activities Affecting Workers (16 January 2025): [verified as issued; currency unverified].** Issued jointly, replacing the October 2016 Antitrust Guidance for Human Resource Professionals, and expanding the frame from per se wage-fixing and no-poach to **compensation benchmarking, wage-setting practices, and the exchange of employment data, particularly through third parties, algorithms or data aggregation tools** (https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-doj-antitrust-guidelines-business-activities-affecting-workers; text: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/p251201antitrustguidelinesbusinessactivitiesaffectingworkers2025.pdf; press release: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-doj-jointly-issue-antitrust-guidelines-business-practices-impact-workers).

**Currency is an open question and we are asking counsel to resolve it, not to assume it.** Secondary practitioner sources reviewed on 19 August 2026 report that the Guidelines have **not been formally withdrawn**, while cautioning against relying on Biden-era policy statements and directing attention to enforcement practice under the current administration; labour-market investigations reportedly remain an enforcement priority (https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/2026-antitrust-year-in-preview-labor-markets.html; https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/one-year-into-trump-20-enforcement-agenda-of-us-antitrust-agencies-continues-to-evolve; https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/publications/2026/04/antitrust-agency-insights-developments-at-the-us-antitrust-enforcement-agencies-first-quarter-2026). Please confirm the Guidelines' current status directly. **[unverified].** `THESIS.md` §9 asserts that under these Guidelines "being a neutral third-party intermediary is **expressly not a defence**." We could not confirm that proposition against the text of the Guidelines. It may be a fair summary of the third-party/data-aggregator passage or it may be an internal overstatement; either way it is currently load-bearing in our own thesis and should be checked.

**EU Horizontal Guidelines (2023/C 259/01), information exchange chapter: [verified in substance].** The Guidelines were verified against the Commission's own text (C(2023) 3445 final, Annex, 1 June 2023, https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-07/2023_revised_horizontal_guidelines_en.pdf). The passage cited by practitioners as **¶408** provides that participants in a reciprocal data-sharing arrangement such as a data pool "should in principle only have access to their own information and the final, aggregated, information of other participants", that technical and practical measures should ensure a participant cannot obtain commercially sensitive information from other participants individually, that management of a pool may be assigned to **a trustee subject to strict confidentiality rules**, and that pool managers should collect only information necessary for the pool's legitimate purpose. The immediately preceding passage describes clean teams and trustees as mechanisms for ensuring information is exchanged "exclusively on a need-to-know basis and **in an aggregated manner**." (Paragraph number taken from practitioner citation: https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2023/global/information-exchange-the-new-eu-regime-for-horizontal-agreements; https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/7debe299/the-new-horizontal-guidelines-information-exchange. The text itself is verified in the primary document; pdftotext extraction drops the marginal paragraph numbers, so counsel should confirm the number.)

The consequence we draw, for correction: the trustee structure the Guidelines contemplate is a trustee that **aggregates**. A trustee that delivers one participant a competitor's individual, identified data, which is exactly what a prior packet is, is not the structure the Guidelines bless, and the presence of an independent administrator does no work on its own. The Guidelines also confirm that agreements to fix wages are treated as restrictions by object, which establishes that labour-market conduct is squarely within Art. 101 even though it is not our conduct.

**UK CMA, *Competing for talent*: [verified, and the closest factual analogue we found].** The CMA's guide for businesses on competition law in recruitment and pay-setting (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/competing-for-talent; guide PDF: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68bffbb38c6d992f23edd75c/competing-for-talent-guide.pdf) identifies information sharing about terms and conditions of employment as one of three principal risks alongside no-poaching and wage-fixing, and gives worked examples. One example, listed among those **more likely to be problematic**, is headed "**Multilateral exchange of information through a third party**":

> In a region of the UK where there are three main employers of workers with particular skills (for example IT engineers), those three companies hire a HR consultancy to collate information about salaries and other terms and conditions of employment (such as bonuses and other benefits) each of them offers. They refer to this as 'benchmarking'. Even though the resulting report only discloses average salaries, because of the small number of employers involved it gives the three employers a good insight into what the others are offering to their highly skilled staff, which they all consider to be useful information when setting salaries and benefits packages.

By contrast, the example the CMA lists as **less likely to be problematic** is a consultancy publishing average salaries for a role across England based on input from 200 companies, where no individual firm can be identified and company-specific information is disclosed to no other party.

We take this as the sharpest available statement of the risk: **a small number of employers, a third-party administrator, and averages only was still the problematic example.** Grain's first market is a small number of hospitality employers in one metropolitan area, with a third-party administrator, and the data is not averaged. It is about named individuals. The only distinction is subject-matter: the CMA example is about pay, and Grain exchanges no pay data. Whether that distinction survives is Question 3(b). Please also confirm the guide's current version and date; practitioner commentary dates the current version to September 2025 and refers to a subsequent update, and we could not reconcile the versions.

**What we have not researched at all.** Philippine and Indian competition law; hub-and-spoke doctrine and its application to a platform that is also a market participant; private treble-damages exposure and class-certification risk in the US; any monopsony theory distinct from information exchange; and whether any Grain conduct could be characterised as facilitating a no-poach or wage-fixing agreement rather than merely an exchange. These are gaps, not positions.

**Our overall working assessment, stated so counsel can demolish it.** The exclusion of compensation from the schema and the worker-gated read path are the two facts we would lead with, and neither is a defence we would want to rely on without an opinion. We think the honest characterisation is that this is an information exchange among competitors with unusually good access controls and unusually bad data characteristics, and that the multi-employer pooling premise may not survive contact with §1 in the form currently designed. If that is right, the answer is needed now, because the premise is the company rather than a feature of it.

## 4. What the answer will drive, and when it is needed

Grain is **pre-launch and pre-revenue**. Nothing described here is in production and no external attesting party has been activated.

The answer bears on **whether the multi-employer pooling premise is viable at all**. This is upstream of the product rather than a constraint on it: the record layer is free forever, so analytics carries all revenue, and analytics is computed across the pooled multi-employer record. If counsel's answer is that a pooled cross-employer performance record among competing local employers cannot be built in this shape, that is a finding about the company, not about a feature, and it is worth vastly more to us now than in eighteen months.

Sequencing:

- **Before the first external attesting party is activated.** Until then every attestation originates inside Grain's own verticals and there is no exchange between independent competitors. Activation of the first external party is the moment the arrangement acquires the structure this brief is about. The registry state machine already gates that activation on other conditions (Brief 2); this opinion should become a further gate.
- **Before any analytics product is sold to a second employer in the same local market.** One customer in a market is not an exchange. Two are. This is the cheapest possible operational trigger and we would like counsel to confirm it is the right one, or to give us a better one.
- **Question 7 is needed first and can be answered fastest.** Architecture, contract terms and output constraints are cheap to build now and expensive to retrofit. This is the same asymmetry that governs the erasure and commitment questions in Briefs 1 and 2. A preliminary answer on 7 ahead of the full opinion would be useful.
- **Question 5(b) closes a named open gap.** `THESIS.md` §10 item 22 records that no trigger has been set for when contract-and-audit neutrality stops being sufficient. Counsel's answer is the intended input to that ruling.
