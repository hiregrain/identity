# IDV/KYC/KYB Vendor Landscape (2026), Evaluated for This Buyer

**Evidence tier — binds nothing.** Research informs decisions and constrains
no implementation. A figure here describes what someone else did or what a
regulator said in one matter; it is not a target, a spec, or a decision.

**Status:** Research memo, not a design doc. Confidence levels stated per claim.
**Buyer profile assumed throughout:** 3-person AI-build-heavy team; verification bought, not built (design 0.1 §1.2); universal self-signup; scale 10⁴ → 10⁶ → 10⁷ verifications/yr; target populations US trades, Philippine ops, Indian workers, plus KYB on attesting parties; sensitive-data payload ban means the ledger must never hold document images.

## TLDR (conclusions first)

1. **Pilot shortlist: Sumsub (global primary) + Persona (US primary / KYB / second source), adding one India-native rail provider (IDfy, Signzy, or HyperVerge class) at India launch.** Sumsub because it is the only major with published self-serve pricing (~$1.35–1.85/check), documented Philippines PhilSys support, an India DigiLocker/Aadhaar eKYC integration, and region-pinnable data storage (Local Data Processing on any AWS region). Persona because it has the most attestation-shaped API (per-check structured results, not opaque pass/fail), is a 2026 Gartner MQ Leader, and carries a credible KYB product for partner vetting. India database rails (PAN, DL, DigiLocker pulls) are cheaper and better through India-native providers than through any global vendor. **Medium-high confidence** on the shortlist logic; pricing figures medium.

2. **The ledger can and should never hold a document image — and the vendor-swap assumption holds.** All majors are processor-holds-evidence by default; the customer can consume results-only and never persist images with every one of them — the variable is *deletion control*, where Persona is best (delete-on-completion default possible, per-data-type retention, field-level deletion, 3-yr outer bound), Stripe has a redact API (~4 days, irreversible) with 3-yr/1-yr-biometric defaults, Veriff prices retention as a tier (3–6-mo default), Sumsub follows client instruction with region-pinned storage. The ledger stores only `{issuer=vendor, method, level, verified_at, vendor_reference_id, evidence_commitment}` — exactly the designed verification-attestation schema (design 0.1 §1.2), vendor-neutral by construction. The incident record makes results-only consumption non-negotiable: Onfido $28.5M, Jumio $7M, and Veriff $4M BIPA settlements, and the Oct 2025 Discord/5CA breach exposing ~70K users' government-ID photos held for age-verification appeals — retained ID images anywhere in the chain are breach surface and biometric-privacy liability. R2 whole-profile deletion must propagate via the vendor's deletion API; a vendor without one is disqualified. **Medium-high confidence** (official docs for Persona/Stripe/Sumsub/Veriff; Onfido/Jumio retention terms are contract-negotiated — verify in contract).

3. **Document-based IDV is measurably weaker than marketed.** DHS RIVTD/RIVR independent testing (2024–Feb 2026): of 7 document-validation systems tested, only 1 met both security and usability targets; 5 of 7 failed false-acceptance thresholds ("none are robust"). Selfie biometrics did better (10 of 16 met baseline), but demographically-similar impostors beat median systems ~11× more often than random impostors, and darker-skin-tone users saw elevated false rejections in at least one tested system. **High confidence** (government testing, not vendor claims). Design consequence: document IDV is one evidence grade among several, never a binary gate — which the leveled-attestation design already accommodates.

4. **Philippines: legally permissive, rail-poor.** PhilSys: ~90.7M registered (~80% of population, Dec 2025) but only ~54.9M plastic cards delivered plus ~48.3M paper ePhilIDs — the 35M+ delivery gap breaks "scan your national ID" for a large minority of workers. BSP Circular 1170 makes PhilID/ePhilID sufficient sole proof of identity, and PhilSys Check (verify.philsys.gov.ph) gives free public cryptographic validation of the PSA-signed QR — but that is signature validation, **not** live biometric match; the fuller eKYC/biometric API is deployed with government relying parties only, private access via PSA accreditation, not self-serve. NBI clearance (~PHP155, in-person biometrics, same-day unless a name-HIT adds 5–10 days) has **no public API** — its QR resolves to the NBI record for scan-and-match. No commercial identity-database-lookup market exists (no PAN/UAN equivalent, no agent network); the whole PH stack is PhilSys QR + NBI QR + document-scan. **High confidence** on numbers and no-API points; **medium** on API-access status.

5. **India: rail-rich and cheap; the binding constraint is legal.** ~99% adult Aadhaar coverage. Post-2018 SC ruling, private entities cannot mandate or freely authenticate Aadhaar; the Aadhaar Good Governance Amendment Rules (Jan 31 2025) opened a ministry-application route, but a foreign/US platform realistically cannot get direct AUA/KUA status. The compliant private paths: Aadhaar Paperless Offline e-KYC (UIDAI-signed XML, designed for the private sector), DigiLocker OAuth pull with user consent (real third-party Requester access; aggregators like Setu/Decentro/HyperVerge resell it), or a licensed Indian sub-processor. Sumsub already wraps the DigiLocker path (`ind_gov_aadhaar_digi`); India-native providers (IDfy, Signzy, Surepass, HyperVerge, AuthBridge, Karza/Perfios) sell PAN (~INR 1–3), DL, EPFO/UAN employment history, and penny-drop bank checks (~INR 1–3) — database checks INR 1–5, DigiLocker/offline-Aadhaar flows INR 5–20 (per-check figures **low confidence**, quote-based). DPDP Rules were notified Nov 14 2025: consent-manager registration and end of soft enforcement Nov 2026, full enforcement May 2027 with penalties to INR 250cr, extraterritorial — a US platform verifying Indian workers is in scope; build consent + deletion now. Aadhaar-derived biometric auth data may not be stored, numbers must be masked to last-4, and eKYC data must be processed/stored in India — India volume needs an India-localized leg regardless of global primary. Fingerprint flows are additionally the wrong choice for this population (worn fingerprints among manual laborers; 2025 parliamentary scrutiny of failure rates). **High confidence** on the legal/regulatory shape.

6. **Skip commercial orchestration layers at this scale; the ledger's own attestation schema *is* the orchestration layer.** Orchestrators sell waterfall routing and case management to compliance teams at enterprise prices (Alloy: ~$12–30K starter rising to $80–200K+ ACV — BENCH); a 3-person team with a vendor-neutral verification-attestation schema gets ~80% of that value from a thin internal `verify(person) → result` adapter per vendor at ~zero cost. Multi-vendor is per-*region* specialization (routing India to IDfy-class pricing vs. $1+ Western IDV), not per-check failover — and that routing is where orchestrators start paying for themselves at multi-region 10⁶+, with AiPrise (emerging-markets-focused, 700+ sources) the most plausible fit and open-source Ballerine the self-host option if one person owns it. **Medium-high confidence.**

7. **KYB: at partner-vetting volume (dozens–hundreds), do not buy a KYB platform.** US FinCEN BOI is effectively dead for this purpose (final rule effective Aug 14 2026 ends domestic reporting and deletes filed data; the database was never open to private parties); EU UBO access is legitimate-interest-gated post-CJEU and untested for a US platform; PH and India have no queryable UBO registers at all. The working stack: manual registry checks (free portals; UK Companies House free API), notarized ownership self-attestation with document collection, pay-as-you-go sanctions screening, and a one-off D&B/LSEG UBO report for any UBO-sensitive deal. Revisit vendors at thousands of parties. **High confidence** on the regulatory facts; **medium** on vendor coverage claims.

8. **Indicative cost:** ~$1–15K/yr at 10⁴ checks on self-serve tiers (Veriff from $0.80, Sumsub $1.35, Persona's free 500/mo tier covers early pilots); at 10⁶, list pricing implies $0.8–1.5M but negotiated blended $0.30–0.75/check is realistic → ~$0.3–0.8M/yr, with India rails ($0.03–0.25/check) pulling the blend down as India share grows; at 10⁷, ~$2–5M/yr blended with aggressive regional routing, and self-hosted components (Regula-class doc SDK) re-open build-vs-buy. Billing basis matters: approved-only vs. all-sessions billing is a 20–40% cost swing on fraud-heavy traffic. **Medium confidence to 10⁶ (benchmark-backed), low past it.**

### 1.2 India-native rail providers (a different category, not competitors to the above)

IDfy, Signzy, HyperVerge, Surepass, Karza (now Perfios), AuthBridge, Bureau, Gridlines, Setu, Decentro: these sell **API access to Indian government/quasi-government rails** — DigiLocker pulls, PAN verification (~INR 1–3), driving licence (Sarathi/Parivahan), voter ID, EPFO/UAN employment-history lookups (formal workers only — unusually relevant to a work ledger), GSTIN, and penny-drop bank verification (~INR 1–3; a strong signal since Indian bank accounts are Aadhaar-KYC'd) — plus face-match tuned on Indian faces and RBI V-CIP compliance flows. Global vendors do not have this access; they OCR the documents instead. Ballpark: database checks INR 1–5, DigiLocker/offline-Aadhaar flows INR 5–20 (**low confidence** on exact numbers — quote-based; the category and order of magnitude are solid). At Indian volume this difference dominates the cost model. Sources: [IDfy](https://www.idfy.com/identity-verification-solutions-by-idfy/api-solutions-overview/), [Surepass](https://surepass.io/identity-verification/), [comparison noting global-vendor gap](https://productgrowth.in/tools/kyc-identity/onfido/).

The Philippines has **no equivalent tier** — no commercial identity-database-lookup market, no PAN/UAN analog, no agent network. PH-document specialists exist (Verihubs, Innov8tif, Didit; Jumio and Shufti also process PH docs), but they wrap document-scan and the public PhilSys QR check, not database rails. GCash/Maya eKYC piggyback (>90% of adults 18–45 hold one) is a plausible future proxy but exposes no public identity API today (**low confidence** it becomes available). **Medium-high confidence** on the structural asymmetry.

### 1.3 Measured quality vs. marketing (the load-bearing skepticism)

Independent government testing is the only trustworthy quality evidence found, and it is sobering:

- **DHS RIVR document validation (reported Feb 2026):** 7 anonymized commercial systems tested with real + fraudulent documents. Only 1 met security and usability targets simultaneously; 4 of 7 failed false-rejection thresholds; 5 of 7 failed false-acceptance thresholds. Assessment: "none are robust." [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202602/dhs-rivr-results-suggest-most-id-document-validation-disastrously-ineffective) **High confidence.**
- **DHS RIVTD selfie biometrics:** 10 of 16 systems met baseline thresholds; demographically-similar impostors achieved ~11× the false-match rate of random impostors at the median system. [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202503/understanding-the-results-of-dhs-sts-rivtd-biometrics-assessment), [DHS framework](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/25_0723_st_quantitative_framework_for_evaluating_remote_identity_validation_systems.pdf) **High confidence.**
- **Demographic equity:** a large-scale study of commercial remote IDV ([arXiv 2409.12318](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.12318)) and NIST's demographic-differential program ([NISTIR 8280](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/reports/demographics/nistir_8280.pdf)) found elevated false rejections for Black/African-American participants and darker skin tones in at least one tested technology; only a minority of tested solutions produced equitable results across race/ethnicity. **High confidence** that differentials exist and vary sharply by vendor; per-vendor current numbers are not public because DHS anonymizes.
- Vendor liveness certifications (iBeta ISO 30107-3 PAD Level 2 — held by iProov (used by Jumio), FaceTec, Yoti, Veridas, others) are real third-party tests but narrow: they certify presentation-attack detection, not end-to-end pass rates or fairness. Treat "iBeta Level 2" as table stakes, not differentiation. **Medium-high confidence.**

Implication: no vendor's published pass-rate or bias marketing should be believed without a pilot on *this* population. The pilot is the measurement.

## 2. Emerging-market reality

### 2.1 Philippines

- **PhilSys rollout (Dec 2025 state):** ~90.7M registered (~80% of population), but only ~54.9M plastic cards delivered plus ~48.3M paper ePhilIDs; the backlog stood at ~36M in early 2025. The ePhilID is downloadable via the eGovPH app and legally co-equal to the card under RA 11055. **High confidence** on numbers. Practical consequence: a "scan your national ID card" flow excludes a large minority of registered workers; the QR-bearing ePhilID (paper or digital) is the artifact to design for. ([Inquirer/PSA earlier milestone](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1880671/more-than-82-million-filipinos-have-registered-for-philsys-ids-says-psa/amp), [PhilID Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_national_identity_card))
- **PhilSys Check (verify.philsys.gov.ph):** free and public today — cryptographic validation of the PSA-signed QR on PhilID/ePhilID, returning active/revoked status and demographics + photo. Critical nuance: this is **QR-signature validation, not live 1:1 biometric match**. The fuller biometric/eKYC API is deployed with *government* relying parties only; private-sector access runs through PSA partnership/accreditation, not self-serve. Known problems: biometric verification failures, name/civil-status errors, banks historically rejecting the PhilID (no signature field). **High confidence** on what PhilSys Check does; **medium** on the private-access status.
- **Regulatory anchor:** BSP Circular 1170 (2023) designates the PhilID (physical, digital, or PSN/PCN alone) as *sufficient* sole proof of identity — no additional document may be demanded. Strongest possible tailwind for a PhilSys-first flow. [Verihubs eKYC guide](https://verihubs.com/ph/blog/ekyc-ph) **Medium-high confidence.**
- **What workers actually hold:** PhilSys has the highest coverage (~80% registered; scannable-artifact possession lower, per the delivery gap). UMID covers formally-employed SSS members — BPO/ops workers overwhelmingly *have SSS numbers* but not all hold the card. Passports and driver's licenses are minority documents (**low confidence** on exact counts); the voter's ID is suspended; barangay certification and PhilHealth ID are secondary-only. Realistic BPO/ops document set: PhilID/ePhilID + UMID + NBI clearance. Any flow assuming passport/DL excludes much of the population. **Medium confidence.**
- **NBI clearance:** the standard employment screen. Online application, ~PHP 155, in-person biometric capture, same-day issuance unless a name-HIT adds 5–10 days; first-time jobseekers exempt from fees (RA 11261). **No public verification API** — the clearance's QR resolves to the NBI record (photo, name, NBI ID) for scan-and-match or portal keying; local screeners (VerifyPros.ph, Veremark, Avvanz, Sterling/First Advantage) wrap this manually. Ledger treatment: an uploaded credential verified by QR-match, or attested by the requiring party who sighted it — never an automated rail. **High confidence.**
- **Failure modes and fallbacks:** registered-but-no-card; worn paper ePhilIDs; OCR/name inconsistencies (compound surnames, civil-status changes — fuzzy name-matching is table stakes). Fallback ladder, descending: (a) PhilSys Check QR — free, cryptographic, the best rail; (b) legacy primary ID doc-scan + liveness; (c) GCash/Maya eKYC piggyback (>90% of adults 18–45) — plausible proxy, **no public identity API today (low confidence it opens)**; (d) SIM Registration Act signals are weak (leaky enforcement); (e) in-person attestation by a company program — which this ledger uniquely treats as a first-class party attestation. There is **no commercial database-lookup market and no agent network** — PH is rail-poor; the whole stack is PhilSys QR + NBI QR + document-scan. **Medium-high confidence.**

### 2.2 India

- **Coverage:** Aadhaar covers ~99% of the adult population (1.3B+ numbers) — the PH's coverage problem does not exist; the *access-regime* problem replaces it. **High confidence.**
- **Legal regime for private use:** post-2018 SC ruling (striking §57 of the Aadhaar Act), private entities cannot mandate or freely run Aadhaar *authentication*. The Aadhaar Good Governance Amendment Rules (Jan 31 2025) opened a ministry-application route to authentication access (UIDAI examines, MeitY approves), but a foreign/US platform realistically cannot obtain direct AUA/KUA status. The three compliant paths: (a) **Aadhaar Paperless Offline e-KYC** — UIDAI-signed XML/ZIP, explicitly designed for private-sector verification; (b) **DigiLocker OAuth pull** of e-Aadhaar (and PAN, DL, education certs, hundreds more) with user consent — third-party Requester access is real (partner registration + OAuth 2.0), and aggregators (Setu, Decentro, Cashfree, HyperVerge) resell it commercially as the standard non-bank KYC pattern; (c) a licensed Indian sub-processor. Aadhaar use must remain voluntary with a non-Aadhaar alternative offered. Sumsub productizes path (b) ([`ind_gov_aadhaar_digi`](https://docs.sumsub.com/reference/india)). **High confidence.**
- **DPDP Act 2023:** Rules notified **Nov 14 2025**, phased: consent-manager registration and end of soft enforcement **Nov 2026**; full enforcement **May 2027**, penalties to INR 250cr. Extraterritorial — a US platform verifying Indian workers is in scope. Build consent flows and deletion now, not at the deadline. Aadhaar-specific hard constraints: no storing biometric authentication data, Aadhaar numbers masked to last-4, eKYC data processed/stored in India (RBI-regulated flows add 5-yr retention). Consequence: India volume needs an **India-localized processing leg** (India-native provider, or Sumsub LDP pinned to an India region) regardless of the global primary. **High confidence.**
- **Biometric reality for this worker population:** Aadhaar *fingerprint* authentication has documented high failure rates for manual laborers (worn fingerprints) and the elderly — bad enough for Parliament's Public Accounts Committee to scrutinize UIDAI in 2025 ([Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202507/high-rates-of-aadhaar-biometric-verification-failure-leads-to-uidai-scrutiny)). Face-based, OTP, and DigiLocker flows avoid this. **High confidence.** For a trades/blue-collar ledger this is decisive: fingerprint-dependent flows would systematically fail exactly the target users.
- **Alternative rails** (via the India-native tier; per-check figures **low confidence**, quote-based): PAN verification ~INR 1–3; driving licence (Sarathi/Parivahan); voter ID; EPFO/UAN employment-history lookups (formal workers only — unusually relevant to a work ledger); GSTIN (KYB); penny-drop bank verification ~INR 1–3 (strong signal — Indian bank accounts are Aadhaar-KYC'd).
- **Blue-collar failure modes:** the #1 issue is a dead or changed Aadhaar-linked mobile → OTP flows fail; then name mismatches across Aadhaar/PAN/bank (transliteration); no PAN or EPFO record for informal workers; worn documents defeating OCR. Fallback ladder: Aadhaar **secure QR** (offline-verifiable, no OTP needed) → offline XML → penny-drop → face-match against the Aadhaar photo → voter ID/DL → assisted verification at a program/partner site → party attestation. Fuzzy name-matching is table stakes. **Medium confidence.**

## 3. Multi-vendor architecture

**Assessed options:**

1. **Single global vendor.** Cheapest to integrate (one adapter), worst on India economics and rails, one throat to choke, real lock-in only if you let vendor IDs/reason-codes leak into your schema. Adequate for the 10⁴ stage only.
2. **Per-region vendors (recommended end-state).** Global vendor for US/generic doc+selfie; India-native provider for Indian rails; PH regional or global-with-PhilSys for PH. Two-to-three thin adapters. This is specialization, not redundancy — each vendor is chosen because it is the *best rail* in its region.
3. **Commercial orchestration layer.** Sells waterfall routing, vendor failover, and case-management UI. The field, concretely: **Alloy** (the reference orchestrator; ~$12–30K starter rising to $80–200K+ ACV — BENCH; built for bank compliance teams, overkill here); **AiPrise** ($12.5M raised Oct 2025, 700+ sources, ML region-routing, explicitly built for India/PH/emerging markets — the most plausible fit *if* an orchestrator is ever bought; pricing unpublished); **Sardine** (fraud-first, enterprise); **Trulioo** (routes to its *own* data network, not vendor-agnostic — lock-in is their data); **Persona Workflows** (within-ecosystem only); **Ballerine** (open-source, self-hostable — images never leave your infra — but repo skews KYB and it needs a dedicated owner); **Regula** (not an orchestrator: an on-prem doc-reader + face SDK license — the "own the pipeline" building block at 10⁷ where per-check fees dominate, at the cost of taking on the document-forensics arms race yourself). Adds a platform fee, a new lock-in (workflow definitions), and enterprise sales friction — to solve a problem this ledger's schema already solves. At 10⁴–10⁵, one primary vendor + a thin internal `verify(person) → result` abstraction captures ~80% of orchestration value at ~zero cost; orchestrators start paying at multi-region 10⁶+, where the value is routing India to IDfy-class pricing instead of $1+ Western IDV — which the per-region-vendors option achieves without the platform. **Medium-high confidence.**

**Does the vendor-swap-is-cheap assumption hold?** Yes, with two verifications and one caveat:

- *Output mapping is real but shallow.* Persona (per-verification `checks[]` arrays), Sumsub (`reviewResult` + structured check payloads and reject labels), and Stripe (`VerificationReport`) all return structured, decomposable results — none forces opaque pass/fail. Mapping any of them into `{issuer, method, level, verified_at, evidence_commitment}` is a per-vendor table of (check type → method, result → level), days of work. ([Persona docs](https://docs.withpersona.com/integration-guide-understanding-a-persona-api-payload), [Sumsub docs](https://docs.sumsub.com/reference/india)) **Medium-high confidence.**
- *Evidence stays with the vendor, and deletion control is the differentiator.* All majors are processor-holds-evidence by default; results-only consumption is possible with every one. Deletion/retention granularity: **Persona** best (delete-on-completion default possible, per-population/per-data-type retention, field-level deletion, 3-yr outer bound — OFFICIAL); **Stripe** redact API (~4 days, irreversible; 3-yr default, biometric 1-yr); **Veriff** retention priced as a tier (3/6-mo default on Essential, +$0.30 for 2-yr; session-deletion API on Premium); **Sumsub** per client instruction, region-pinned via Local Data Processing; **Onfido/Jumio/Incode** contract-negotiated — verify in the DPA. The incident record is why this matters: Onfido $28.5M, Jumio $7M, Veriff $4M BIPA settlements; 2024 BIPA suits over missing public retention/destruction schedules; the Oct 2025 Discord/5CA breach exposed ~70K users' government-ID photos held for age-verification appeals. BIPA liability follows whoever "collects" — put written retention/destruction schedules in the DPA. Nothing requires the customer to ingest images; the customer chooses whether to ever call media-download endpoints. **Don't.** **Medium-high confidence** (official docs for the first four; contract items flagged).
- *Caveat — history is non-portable.* Swapping vendors moves *future* checks only; past evidence stays retrievable only while the old vendor contract (or its wind-down terms) lives. The attestation and its `evidence_commitment` survive forever; the underlying media may not. Acceptable — the ledger's unit of truth is the attestation, not the JPEG — but say it out loud in party agreements.

**Recommended shape:** an internal `VerificationProvider` adapter interface (request check, receive webhook, map to verification attestation, forward deletion). Each vendor is a **registered attesting party** in the trust registry (design 0.1 §1.2 already says this), so vendor output enters the ledger through the same signed-attestation front door as everything else — the ledger signs the attestation recording what the vendor asserted, with the vendor as named issuer.

## 4. KYB for attesting-party vetting

(Condensed from the KYB sub-research; volumes here are dozens–hundreds of partners, not onboarding-scale.)

- **Vendors:** Middesk (US-only, all-50-states SoS + IRS TIN, best US depth, low-five-figure minimums, weak UBO — medium conf.); Trulioo (broadest global claim, ~70% PH UBO coverage claimed Apr 2026 — vendor-defined, low-moderate conf.; India detail unpublished; custom quotes); Sumsub KYB (~$1.35–1.85 floor, 200+ countries, weaker US registry depth — usable as the same-vendor add-on); Persona KYB (~30 countries automated, ~100 incl. APAC in early access — PH/India tier unconfirmed); D&B/LSEG (only real private-company UBO approximation for PH/India; enterprise-priced, buy per-report); OpenCorporates (aggregation not verification, £2,250–12,000/yr, no UBO, staleness reports — low-moderate conf.); Companies House UK (free API incl. PSC/UBO register — gold standard).
- **Direct registry reality (high conf.):** PH SEC/DTI — free manual portals, no public API; India MCA — free master data (+ data.gov.in mirror), GSTIN lookup free manually / cheap via GSP intermediaries; EU BRIS — free manual cross-border checks, no public API; US — free SoS web search per state, fragmented APIs (aggregators ~$0.03/lookup). A 3-person team can clear dozens-to-hundreds of vetting checks manually at ~15–45 min/company.
- **UBO reality (high conf. on regulation):** US FinCEN BOI is gone for this purpose — the final rule effective **Aug 14 2026** permanently ends domestic BOI reporting and deletes previously filed domestic data; the database was never accessible to private parties anyway ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16576)). EU: public access dead post-CJEU (2022); AMLD6 restores legitimate-interest access with timelines from Nov 2026, but whether a US worker-platform qualifies is member-state-dependent and untested. PH and India: no queryable UBO registers (PH GIS filings and India BEN-2 are filed, not searchable).
- **Recommendation:** manual registry checks + notarized UBO **self-attestation** with document collection + pay-as-you-go sanctions/adverse-media screening (Sumsub per-check or OpenSanctions) + one-off D&B/LSEG report for UBO-sensitive deals. No KYB platform subscription until the registry holds thousands of parties or needs continuous monitoring. This also fits the design: party vetting evidence lives in the trust registry (design 0.1 §3.1), which is tiered and human-reviewed anyway.

## 5. Bottom line

### 5.1 Pilot shortlist

1. **Sumsub — global primary.** Only major with: published self-serve pricing (start without a sales cycle — fits a 3-person team), PhilSys support, a productized India DigiLocker eKYC flow, region-pinnable data processing (EU/India/APAC via any AWS region), and a KYB add-on for overflow party-vetting checks. Risks: crypto-heavy customer base and aggressive marketing — validate pass rates on the actual PH/IN worker population before scaling; US registry/database depth weaker than US-native options.
2. **Persona — US primary and second source.** 2026 Gartner MQ Leader, the most attestation-shaped API in the market (structured `checks[]` per verification), the best deletion/retention control of any major (delete-on-completion, field-level deletion), a **free tier of 500 govt-ID+selfie checks/month** that makes the US pilot nearly free, workflow tooling that substitutes for an orchestrator, and KYB for the US/EU vetting tier. Risks: beyond free tier pricing is quote-only with enterprise minimums (median contract ~$180K/yr per buyer benchmarks — negotiate hard or stay inside the free tier until US volume justifies it); India/PH depth is doc-OCR, not rails; no documented India residency.
3. **India-native rail provider (IDfy / Signzy / HyperVerge / Surepass class) — add at India launch.** Selected by bake-off on DigiLocker + PAN + DL + EPFO/UAN rails, per-call price, face-match performance on Indian faces, and India-localized processing (a DPDP/Aadhaar requirement, not a preference). Not chosen now: this tier is quote-based and moving; choosing today buys nothing.

Priced fallback worth naming: **Veriff Essential at $0.80/check ($49/mo)** is the published cost floor among majors if Sumsub pilot economics disappoint — with the caveat of a 3-month default retention tier and thinner structured output.

Two pilots (Sumsub broad, Persona US) running against the same adapter interface *is itself* the vendor-swap test — if the second adapter takes more than days, the schema assumption failed and should be fixed while it's cheap.

### 5.2 The no-images-in-ledger data flow

```
worker device ── vendor SDK/hosted flow ──► vendor cloud (images, selfie,
      │                                     liveness video live HERE,
      │                                     retention configured per contract,
      │                                     region-pinned where required)
      │                                             │ webhook: structured result
      ▼                                             ▼
ledger backend ◄────────────── {result, checks[], vendor_reference_id}
      │
      ├─► verification attestation (payload plane): issuer=vendor(registry id),
      │     method, level, verified_at, expires/decays_at, vendor_reference_id
      ├─► evidence_commitment (integrity spine): hash of the vendor's structured
      │     result — NOT of any image the ledger never possessed
      └─► NEVER calls vendor media-download endpoints; images never transit
            ledger infrastructure

R2 profile deletion ──► adapter forwards deletion request to vendor's
                        GDPR-deletion API for that applicant; logged as event
```

Sensitive extracted fields (document numbers, DOB) stay vendor-side too: the attestation records that a document of type X was verified at level Y — the sensitive-data payload ban (design 0.1 §2.2) already prohibits carrying the numbers, and no vendor forces you to.

### 5.3 Indicative cost model (confidence degrades with scale)

| Stage | Volume/yr | Blended $/check | Annual | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot | 10⁴ | $0.80–1.85 published self-serve (Veriff Essential $0.80 / Sumsub $1.35–1.85 / Stripe ~$1.50); Persona free tier absorbs up to 6K US checks/yr; platform minimums $0.6–3.6K/yr | **~$1–15K** | OFFICIAL published rates; medium-high conf. |
| Scale | 10⁶ | List implies $0.80–1.50; negotiated blended **$0.30–0.75** realistic; India rails ($0.03–0.25) pull the blend down as India share grows; approved-only vs. all-sessions billing swings cost 20–40% on fraudy traffic | **~$0.3–0.8M** | BENCH volume curves (Jumio $0.75–2 at 500K+) + ANEC inference; medium-low conf. |
| Mass | 10⁷ | $0.15–0.50 blended, only with heavy regional routing + non-doc rails; US doc+selfie stays ≥$0.50 | **~$2–5M** | Extrapolation; low conf. — pricing is bespoke and build-vs-buy on components (self-hosted Regula-class doc SDK) re-opens |
| KYB | 10²–10³ parties | ~$0 marginal + analyst time (~15–45 min/company); screening cents/check; occasional $100s/UBO report | **<$20K** | High conf. at this volume |

Structural note: **per-verification cost is not per-person cost.** Progressive verification means one person may generate several checks over a career (initial + step-ups + re-verification on decay), but also that most of the 10⁷ identities at mass scale sit at low assurance cheaply — the leveled-attestation design is itself the cost-control mechanism: buy high-cost verification only where a party or vertical needs the level.

### 5.4 Design constraints (binding recommendations)

1. **The verification attestation must carry `{issuer, method, level, verified_at, expires_or_decays_at, vendor_reference_id, evidence_commitment}` and nothing image-shaped.** `vendor_reference_id` is the pointer into the vendor's evidence store; `method` is a ledger-owned vocabulary (doc-scan, doc+selfie-liveness, government-eKYC, database-match, channel-OTP, party-assisted) mapped from vendor check types — never the vendor's raw check-type strings, or swap costs come back.
2. **Vendors are registered attesting parties** (already in design 0.1) — same registry, lifecycle, and suspension machinery. A vendor whose measured quality collapses (see DHS results) is *suspended in the registry*, downgrading read-time weight on its attestations without touching history. This is the vendor-quality control the industry lacks.
3. **Never ingest verification media.** No media-download calls, no image fields in any schema, ever. Enforced in the adapter interface, not by policy memo.
4. **Deletion propagation is part of the adapter contract.** R2 whole-profile deletion triggers vendor-side deletion via API for every vendor that has ever verified the person; the forward event is logged on the spine. A vendor without a deletion API is disqualified at selection.
5. **Method-level fallback ladders per region, designed for measured failure.** Document IDV fails a material fraction of legitimate users (DHS: 4/7 systems failed FRR targets) with demographic skew. Every region needs a defined ladder (e.g., PH: ePhilID QR → legacy doc+liveness → party-assisted; IN: DigiLocker eKYC → doc+face → OTP/assisted) and a **party-assisted verification** method grade — this ledger's unique advantage is that its program partners can be verification issuers of last resort.
6. **No fingerprint-dependent flows for this population.** Worn fingerprints among manual laborers are a documented systematic failure (Aadhaar record). Face + liveness, OTP, and eKYC pulls only.
7. **India: consent-based DigiLocker/offline-eKYC rails only, with a voluntary non-Aadhaar alternative; India-localized processing leg; DPDP consent + deletion built before the Nov 2026 / May 2027 enforcement dates.** Never architect anything that touches raw Aadhaar numbers (masked-to-last-4 is a legal requirement, and the sensitive-data payload ban already forbids carrying them).
8. **Instrument pass rates by document type, region, and demographic proxy from the first pilot check.** Vendor claims are unverifiable ex ante and the DHS record says the variance between vendors is enormous; the pilot's measured FRR/FAR — not the MQ quadrant — picks the scale vendor. Budget the pilot as a measurement exercise, not a procurement formality.
